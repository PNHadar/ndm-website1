Epilepsy & Neuromodulation Team

Neurophysiology of Epilepsy

The fundamental mechanisms of seizures and epilepsy remain largely a mystery. While we understand that seizures involve excessive synchronization of neurons, the processes initiating and propagating these events are highly complex. Our team utilizes microelectrodes to record individual neuronal activity and small networks, examining these dynamics at the resolution of millimeters and milliseconds. By directly measuring neuronal firing during human seizures, we can uncover patterns of initiation and propagation, driving toward more precise diagnostic and therapeutic interventions.

Neuromodulation of the Human Brain

For decades, neuromodulation has been pivotal in both understanding brain function and treating disease. From foundational studies of motor mapping to contemporary therapies like Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) for Parkinson's disease and responsive neurostimulation for epilepsy, direct electrical stimulation is a powerful tool.

We employ direct electrical cortical stimulation to systematically study its effects on brain networks, particularly across the cortex. Combining this technique with advanced imaging, we aim to map functional connectivity and design personalized, responsive stimulation therapies for patients with treatment-resistant neuropsychiatric disorders.

Cortical and Subcortical Oscillations

Brain waves, or neural oscillations, are critical components of neural communication and processing. From fast gamma ripples to slow delta waves, these rhythmic patterns reflect the coordinated activity of vast neural populations.

Our team investigates how these oscillations vary across different cortical layers and brain states. We explore how pathological oscillations drive epileptic events and how healthy rhythms support cognition, striving to map the language of the brain.
