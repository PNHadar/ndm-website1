Cognition & Sleep Team

Understanding Sleep and Dreams

We spend a third of our lives asleep, a vital physiological process for learning, memory, and overall well-being. However, the exact neural mechanisms defining sleep stages and dream generation remain elusive. Our research utilizes invasive and non-invasive recordings to track how the cortex enters and exits sleep states. We examine the generation of sleep spindles, slow waves, and K-complexes—signatures of sleep that play crucial roles in memory consolidation and synaptic homeostasis. By studying micro-awakenings and localized sleep phenomena, we gain profound insights into how the brain restores itself.

Human Cognition

Cognition encompasses the myriad processes by which the brain processes sensory inputs, forms thoughts, accesses memories, and executes complex behaviors. Utilizing intracranial recordings from patients undergoing epilepsy monitoring, we can directly observe cognition in action with unmatched temporal and spatial resolution.

We design specialized behavioral tasks to probe memory formation, decision-making, and sensory processing. By decoding single-unit activity alongside local field potentials, we seek to uncover the fundamental computational principles of the human mind and how these networks fail in cognitive disorders.
